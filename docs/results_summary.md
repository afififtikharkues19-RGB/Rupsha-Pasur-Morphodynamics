# Core inferential results

## Corridor-wide distributed-lag response

The distributed-lag analysis identified its clearest
corridor-wide curvature–migration association at **2.0B
upstream**. The posterior mean effect was
**-0.085 m/yr per +1 SD curvature**
(95% CrI **-0.180 to -0.019 m/yr**;
P[outward] = **0.993**).

Under the locked geometric convention, a negative coefficient
represents migration in the direction opposite to the sign of
curvature and therefore corresponds to **outward-aligned
migration**.

## Spatial consistency of the 2.0B signal

The 2.0B posterior mean was negative in **10 of
12 spatial blocks (83.3%)**.
However, only **3 blocks** had P(outward) >= 0.90 and no
individual block had a 95% credible interval entirely below
zero.

The strongest local posterior support at 2.0B occurred in
**B06**, where the posterior mean was
**-0.278 m/yr per +1 SD curvature** and
P(outward) was **0.964**.

Accordingly, the corridor-wide 2.0B result is interpreted as a
**spatially widespread outward-aligned tendency with substantial
local uncertainty**, rather than as a small number of reaches
dominating the global signal.

## Broader spatial heterogeneity

Across the full 0–2.5B distributed-lag domain, locally resolved
effects occurred at different spatial offsets and included both
outward- and inward-aligned responses. Thus, the dominant
corridor-wide 2.0B pattern coexists with reach-scale variation in
the magnitude, direction, and apparent spatial offset of
curvature–migration coupling.

## Claim boundary

The analysis supports a conditional association between
antecedent channel geometry and subsequent signed lateral
migration. It does not establish a causal relationship or
out-of-sample predictive skill.
